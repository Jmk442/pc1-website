Script · Opening
You Press Enter. Then What?

You're sitting in class. You open ChatGPT on your laptop. You type:

"Tell me about space"

You press Enter. An answer appears in a few seconds. Easy.

But what actually happened in those few seconds? Where did your words go? What did the computer do with them? And why does it matter?

Let's follow your question on its journey.

There are four steps that happen every single time you use AI. Whether you're asking about homework, writing a story, or looking something up.

But here's the part most people never think about: what happens next?

In the time it takes you to blink — usually less than a second — your question travels from your device, through the internet, to a data centre that might be on the other side of the world. Inside that data centre, something extraordinary happens. A machine — a very large, very fast, very power-hungry machine — reads your words and produces an answer.

This chapter is about that machine. What it does. What it costs. And why the way you write your question matters more than you might think.

There are four steps that happen every time you use AI. Every single time. Whether you're asking about homework, writing a story, or looking something up.

Script · Step 1
Step 1 — The Breaking-Apart

The first thing AI does with your words is break them apart. Not into letters. Not exactly into words either. Into something called tokens.

A token is a chunk of text — sometimes a whole word, sometimes part of one. "Running" might become two tokens: "run" and "ning." "Unbelievable" might become three.

Why does this matter? Because the AI doesn't read the way you read. It doesn't understand sentences the way you do. It processes tokens — one at a time, in sequence — and builds a mathematical picture of what you're asking.

Step 1: Tokenisation. Your words are broken into pieces the AI can process.

Script · Steps 2 & 3
Steps 2 & 3 — The Search and The Making

Step 2: The Search

Once the AI has your tokens, it searches its knowledge. But "searches" isn't quite the right word — it's more like... calculating. The AI was trained on an enormous amount of text from the internet, books, articles, and more. That training taught it which words tend to follow which other words, in which contexts.

When it reads your tokens, it calculates: given everything I know, given all the patterns I've learned — what should come next? And then next? And then next?

It's not looking something up. It's building an answer, one token at a time.

Step 3: The Making

This is where the electricity really gets used. The AI's answer isn't stored somewhere waiting to be found — it's generated fresh, every time. The machine runs millions of calculations to produce each token of your answer.

For a simple question, that might be a hundred tokens. For a long one, it could be thousands.

Each calculation uses electricity. Google has measured it: on average, one AI prompt uses about 0.24 watt-hours of electricity and 0.26 millilitres of water — water used to cool the servers.

That doesn't sound like much. But multiply it by 200 million people, asking questions every day.

Script · Step 4 & Key Insight
Step 4 — The Return. And Why It Matters.

Step 4: The Return

The answer travels back — through the internet, to your device — and appears on your screen. Usually in less than a second.

That's the full journey. Tokenise. Calculate. Generate. Return.

Four steps. Every single time you use AI.

Here's the key insight — and it's the reason this book exists.

The quality of the question you ask changes how hard the machine has to work.

A vague question — "write me an essay" — makes the AI guess. It guesses the topic, the length, the audience, the tone, the format. Each guess uses computing power. And when the answer comes back wrong, you ask again. And again. And again.

Each of those extra attempts is real electricity. Real water. Real cost.

A precise, structured question — one that tells the AI exactly what you need — requires far fewer calculations. The AI doesn't have to guess. It gets it right the first time.

Researchers have measured this. Structured prompting reduces the energy AI uses by 32 to 48 percent.

Not by building better hardware. Not by switching to solar power. Just by asking better questions.

That's what C.R.A.F.T. is. And that's what this book teaches.
