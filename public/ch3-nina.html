Script · The Invisible Machine
The Infrastructure Behind Every Prompt

Every AI interaction depends on physical infrastructure that most users never see.

When you submit a prompt, your text travels to a data centre — a facility housing thousands of high-performance servers, operating continuously, processing requests from users across the world simultaneously. These buildings are not small. A hyperscale data centre can span tens of thousands of square metres. They require two resources in constant supply: electricity to power the computation, and water to dissipate the heat that computation generates.

There are more than 770 hyperscale data centres either operational or under construction globally. Together, they form the physical backbone of every AI interaction, every search query, every cloud service.

They are invisible to the people using them. They are not, however, without consequence.

Script · The Scale of the Problem
How Big Is the Infrastructure Footprint?

The International Energy Agency's 2025 Electricity report places global data centre electricity consumption at approximately 415 terawatt-hours in 2024 — representing 1.5 percent of total global electricity demand.

Goldman Sachs projects a 165 percent increase in data centre power demand by 2030.

A single large data centre can use up to 5 million gallons of water per day.

Cornell University researchers estimate that by 2030, AI alone will produce 24 to 44 million metric tonnes of CO₂ per year.

These figures describe current trajectory, not worst case. They reflect what happens if the demand-side behaviour of AI users does not change — if people continue prompting without structure, generating retries, driving unnecessary compute.

Script · The Infrastructure Trajectory
Why This Is Getting Bigger

The IEA's assessment places current data centre energy use at 1.5 percent of global electricity demand. That figure is expected to rise significantly before the end of the decade.

The growth is driven by two forces: expansion of AI capability, and expansion of AI adoption. More users. More interactions. More infrastructure required to process them.

Over 770 hyperscale data centres are either operational or in the construction pipeline globally. Each represents a long-term capital commitment to infrastructure that will run for decades. The energy demand they represent is not temporary.

What makes this moment significant is that the growth curve can be influenced from the demand side — through changes in how users interact with AI systems, not just how those systems are built.

Script · The Part You Control
The Software Lever

The infrastructure trajectory described in this chapter is the supply-side problem. More servers. More cooling. More power.

But there is a demand-side lever that operates independently of all of it.

Podder et al. (2026) demonstrated that structured prompting reduces inference energy consumption by 32 to 48 percent across multiple large language models. This reduction requires no capital expenditure. No new hardware. No regulatory mandate. No technological breakthrough.

It has five distinctive properties. It works immediately. It is model-agnostic. It scales through education. And it is co-beneficial — improving output quality and reducing environmental cost simultaneously.

The infrastructure behind AI will continue to grow. But how hard that infrastructure has to work — prompt by prompt, user by user — is something that education can change.
