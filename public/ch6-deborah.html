Script · Opening
One Word Changes Everything

One word changes everything.

Not a long word. Not a complicated word. A verb. The action word in your prompt is the single most powerful instruction you give to AI. It tells the machine exactly what kind of thinking to do.

Watch this:

  • "Tell me about volcanoes" → You get a wall of text. Everything the AI knows about volcanoes, dumped on your screen. Most of it isn't what you needed.
  • "Compare the three types of volcanoes in a table" → You get a clean table with shield, composite, and cinder cone side by side. Exactly what your homework asked for.

Same topic. Different verb. Completely different result.

Why does the verb matter so much?

Because AI responds to instructions literally. When you say "tell me about," the AI interprets that as "produce general information." It doesn't know if you want a summary, a comparison, an analysis, or a list. So it produces a bit of everything — and you end up scrolling through paragraphs of stuff you didn't ask for, looking for the one part that's actually useful.

Then you try again. Another prompt. More electricity. More wasted compute.

🔄 Adapt — The Power Verbs Table

Ten Verbs That Change Everything — read as spoken list

The book has a table of 10 action verbs with explanations. Read the spoken version below — pause slightly between each verb so the listener can absorb it.

Here are ten action verbs that trigger ten different types of thinking.

List — items in order, no explanation. Best for quick reference and brainstorming.

Summarise — key points only, short. Best for study notes and overviews.

Compare — side-by-side differences. Best for analysis and decision-making.

Explain — step-by-step understanding. Best for learning new concepts.

Analyse — break apart and examine the pieces. Best for deep study and essays.

Evaluate — judge quality, weigh pros and cons. Best for reviews and arguments.

Create — build something new. Best for stories, plans, and designs.

Classify — sort into categories. Best for organising information.

Predict — project what might happen. Best for science and planning.

Justify — give evidence-backed reasons. Best for persuasive writing and debate.
Script · The One-Verb Rule
The One-Verb Rule

The rule is simple: one prompt, one verb. If you find yourself writing "tell me about and also compare and maybe list," you're asking three questions in one. The AI will try to do all three, do none of them well, and you'll retry.

One verb. One task. One good result.

How action builds on what came before:

In Chapter 4, you set the context — what world the AI is working in. In Chapter 5, you set the role — who the AI should be. Now you're giving that expert, in that world, a specific job to do.

  • Context = the classroom
  • Role = the teacher
  • Action = "explain plate tectonics using a diagram"

Without action, you have a teacher standing in a classroom with nothing to teach. The verb is the lesson plan.
