Script · Opening
The Invisible Machine

You can't see it. You can't hear it. But every time you use AI, a building the size of a shopping centre is working for you.

That building is called a data centre. And there are thousands of them, all around the world, running 24 hours a day.

Script · The Scale
How Big Is the Problem?

Electricity: In 2024, all the data centres in the world used about 415 terawatt-hours of electricity. That's about 1.5% of all the electricity on the entire planet.

Australia's total electricity consumption is about 250 terawatt-hours per year. So the world's data centres use more electricity than all of Australia. Every year.

And it's getting bigger. Goldman Sachs predicts data centre electricity demand will rise 165% by 2030.

Water: A single large data centre can use up to 5 million gallons of water per day.

Carbon: Researchers at Cornell University estimate that by 2030, AI alone will produce 24 to 44 million metric tonnes of CO₂ per year.

Script · The Solution
The Part You Control

You can't change how data centres are built. But you can change how hard they have to work.

Researchers have measured this precisely. A landmark study published in 2026 found that structured prompting reduces the energy AI uses by 32 to 48 percent. Not by building better hardware. Not by switching to solar panels. Just by asking better questions.

That's why the way you write a prompt matters. Not just for you — but for the machines working for you.
