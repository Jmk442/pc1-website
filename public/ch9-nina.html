Script · The Complete System
Five Elements. One Pipeline.

The C.R.A.F.T. framework is not a set of five separate tools. It is a single structured methodology with five interdependent elements. Each element operates on the output of the previous one, progressively constraining the model's generation pathway toward a single, high-quality response.

Context narrows the semantic search space. Role adds perspective within that space. Action directs a specific cognitive operation. Format specifies the structural output. Tone calibrates the register to the intended audience.

Used in sequence, these elements do not merely improve the output — they eliminate the categories of ambiguity that drive retry behaviour. The model no longer needs to guess the domain, the expertise level, the task, the structure, or the audience. All of it is specified.

The result: output accepted on first generation at rates exceeding 85 percent. An 83 to 95 percent reduction in the model's inference search space. A 32 to 48 percent reduction in energy consumption per task.

This is not a theory. It is measurable. And it requires nothing more than structured communication.

🔄 Adapt — C.R.A.F.T. Build — Spoken Element by Element

Building a Prompt — One Element at a Time

Source: the C.R.A.F.T. element table from Chapter 9. TABLE IS CONTENT — read as a spoken build through each element. Example adapted from Australian Year 7 geography → neutral professional research context.

Let us build a complete C.R.A.F.T. prompt together.

Context: A policy team researching the environmental impact of AI infrastructure in Europe.

Role: You are a senior energy policy analyst with expertise in digital infrastructure and carbon accounting.

Action: Compare energy consumption per request across three categories of AI workload — text generation, image generation, and complex reasoning tasks.

Format: A concise report of 400 words, with a comparison table and a two-sentence executive summary at the top.

Tone: Formal and evidence-aware — appropriate for a policymaker audience.

Now combine them: 'You are a senior energy policy analyst with expertise in digital infrastructure and carbon accounting. For a European policy team researching the environmental impact of AI infrastructure, compare energy consumption per request across text generation, image generation, and complex reasoning tasks. Present this as a 400-word report with a comparison table and a two-sentence executive summary at the top. Use a formal, evidence-aware tone appropriate for a policymaker audience.'

One prompt. All five elements. Ready on the first attempt.
