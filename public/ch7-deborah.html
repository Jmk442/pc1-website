Script · Opening
Shaping the Output

You wouldn't use a world map to find your classroom.

If someone asked you for directions to Room 12B, you'd want a simple set of steps: "Go left, second door on the right." You wouldn't want a satellite photo of the entire school. The information might be in there somewhere, but the shape is wrong for the job.

Format is the F in C.R.A.F.T., and it answers one question: What shape should the answer take?

AI can give you the same information in dozens of different shapes:

  • A paragraph — for reading and understanding
  • A table — for comparing things side by side
  • A list — for quick reference
  • A step-by-step guide — for following instructions
  • A dot-point summary — for study notes

The problem? If you don't tell the AI which shape you want, it picks for you. And it almost always picks a paragraph. A long one. With an introduction, a body, a conclusion, and sometimes a polite "I hope this helps!" at the end.

Then you read it, realise you needed a table, and type: "Can you put that in a table instead?"

That second prompt is a full regeneration. The AI doesn't just rearrange the words — it starts from scratch. All the energy from the first attempt? Gone. Wasted.

🔄 Adapt — Without Format vs With Format

The Contrast — read as spoken comparison

The book has a table comparing two approaches. Read the spoken version below.

Without format: "Explain the differences between renewable and non-renewable energy." Result — five paragraphs mixing definitions, examples, and opinions. You need to read all of it to find the comparisons buried in the text.

With format: "Compare renewable and non-renewable energy in a table with columns for source, cost, environmental impact, and availability." Result — a clean table with exactly the four columns you asked for. You can scan it in ten seconds and paste it straight into your assignment.
Script · The Energy Equation
Why Format Saves Energy

Every time you ask AI to reformat something, it generates the entire response again from scratch. That means:

  • Prompt 1: "Explain renewable energy" → 800 tokens generated
  • Prompt 2: "Put that in a table" → 400 tokens generated
  • Total: 1,200 tokens for a 400-token result

If you'd specified the table format in your first prompt, you'd have spent 400 tokens total. That's a 67 percent reduction in compute — from one extra line in your prompt.

At Google's reported rate of 0.24 watt-hours per prompt, that saved retry is real electricity. Multiply by millions of users reformatting every day, and the waste is enormous.

Choosing the right format — think about what you're going to do with the answer:

  • Studying for a test? Use dot-point summaries — quick to scan, easy to memorise.
  • Comparing two or more things? Use a table — side-by-side makes differences obvious.
  • Following a process? Use numbered steps — clear order, nothing skipped.
  • Writing an essay? Use a paragraph with topic sentences — flows naturally.
  • Giving a presentation? Use slide-style dot points — short phrases, not full sentences.
