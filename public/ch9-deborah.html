You Know All Five Letters. Time to Use Them.

You now know all five letters. Time to use them.

C.R.A.F.T. isn't five separate things — it's one process with five steps. Each step makes the next one work better. And when you put all five together, something happens that's bigger than any single step: the AI gets it right the first time.

Let's build a complete C.R.A.F.T. prompt from scratch for a real school assignment.

The assignment: Your Year 7 Geography teacher asks you to write 300 words about how water is used in different industries.

The bad prompt — no C.R.A.F.T.:

"Write about water use in industry"

What you'd get: Probably 800 words covering agriculture, manufacturing, mining, cooling, cleaning, and a dozen other things. Some of it from countries you've never studied. No clear structure. You'd read it, highlight the useful bits, delete the rest, and try again.

🔄 Adapt — The C.R.A.F.T. Element Table

Building the Prompt — read as spoken build

The book has a table showing each C.R.A.F.T. element and the input for this assignment. Read it as a spoken build-through, element by element.

Here's the C.R.A.F.T. prompt, built step by step.

Context — Year 7 Geography, Australian Curriculum, water resources unit.

Role — You are a Year 7 Geography teacher who uses real Australian examples.

Action — Compare water use across three Australian industries.

Format — 300 words with a comparison table and one-paragraph conclusion.

Tone — Clear and engaging, suitable for a 12-year-old reader.
Script · The Full Prompt
The Full Prompt

Put it all together, and the prompt reads like this:

"You are a Year 7 Geography teacher who uses real Australian examples. For a Year 7 class studying water resources under the Australian Curriculum, compare water use across three Australian industries. Present this as 300 words with a comparison table and a one-paragraph conclusion. Use a clear and engaging tone suitable for a 12-year-old reader."

What you'd get: Exactly 300 words. A clean table comparing agriculture, mining, and manufacturing in Australia. A conclusion that ties it together. First attempt. No retry. Ready to submit.

The Numbers — read headline figures only

The book has a side-by-side metrics table comparing the bad prompt vs the C.R.A.F.T. prompt. Read the headline numbers only.

The numbers tell the story simply.

Bad prompt: around 800 words generated, two to three retries, 3,200 tokens total, 0.72 watt-hours of energy, fifteen-plus minutes of your time.

C.R.A.F.T. prompt: around 300 words, zero retries, 400 tokens, 0.24 watt-hours, two minutes.

That's a 67 percent energy reduction and an 87 percent time reduction — from spending 30 seconds thinking before you type.
