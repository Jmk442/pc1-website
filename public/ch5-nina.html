Script · The Mechanics of Role-Priming

What Happens When You Assign a Role

Role assignment in large language models is not a stylistic choice. It is a precise mechanism that alters how the model processes and generates a response.

When a prompt specifies 'Act as a senior environmental policy analyst,' the model's attention mechanism shifts weight toward token sequences associated with that domain's discourse patterns — its terminology, its reasoning structures, its output conventions. The model draws from a constrained vocabulary distribution. It activates structural patterns typical of that expertise. It suppresses token sequences that do not fit the specified role.

This is observable in output. A clinician produces structured clinical notes. A legal professional produces numbered-paragraph submissions. An analyst produces executive summaries with supporting detail. The model is not simulating expertise — it is activating training data that corresponds to the specified expertise.

Role without context is still a person with no specific situation to address. But role with context — as Chapter 4 established — inherits the domain already defined, and adds perspective. Now the model operates as a particular expert, within a particular world, ready to receive a specific task.

The Difference a Role Makes

Here is the contrast.

Context only: 'Write about photosynthesis for a Year 11 biology class.'

The result is accurate. Textbook-style. Reads like a reference article. Technically complete. Not particularly engaging. You probably read it and decide it needs work.

Context plus role: 'You are a Year 11 biology teacher who uses everyday analogies to make abstract processes concrete. Write about photosynthesis for your class.'

The result is different. The teacher uses a kitchen as an analogy — sunlight is the oven, water and CO₂ are the ingredients, glucose is what the plant makes. Students get it immediately. It is the same information. The role shaped how it was communicated.

The first answer is correct. The second answer is useful. The difference is the role.

Script · Choosing the Right Role

Three Rules for Effective Role Assignment

Three principles guide effective role assignment.

The first: match the role to your need. If you want an accessible explanation, specify a teacher. If you want analytical depth, specify a researcher. If you want practical advice, specify a professional who gives that kind of advice — not a generic expert, but a specific one.

The second: be specific. 'Act as a doctor' provides some improvement over no role. 'Act as a general practitioner with fifteen years of experience in chronic disease management' provides substantially more. The more precisely the role constrains the model's persona, the more efficiently it generates relevant output.

The third: stack it on context. Role only works well when context is already set. A teacher without a subject is a persona without a purpose. With context defined, role inherits those boundaries and adds a perspective from which to address them.

Three Headline Figures

Three figures from the role-priming efficiency data.

Without a role: an average of 2.4 retries per task. Tokens per useful output: 1,840.

With a specific role — domain, seniority, and focus specified: an average of 0.6 retries per task. Tokens per useful output: 680.

That is a 75 percent reduction in retries and a 63 percent reduction in tokens — from one additional line of instruction in the prompt.

Script · Role and the Pipeline

How Role Fits the C.R.A.F.T. System

Role is the second element in C.R.A.F.T. because it requires context to function precisely. Without context, a role is a persona with nothing specific to address. With context already established, role inherits those boundaries and adds expertise.

The compounding effect begins here. Context narrowed the semantic search space. Role now narrows it further — not just to a domain, but to a specific perspective within that domain. The model's generation pathway becomes progressively more constrained with each element applied.

In Chapter 6, the action verb will direct that role-primed, context-aware model toward a precise task. But first, role must be clear. Because a teacher cannot teach without knowing who they are speaking to — and a model cannot perform a role effectively without a context to perform it in.
